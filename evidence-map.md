# Evidence map for the "ladder" practice app

Written 2026-11-06. Every DOI below was resolved on Crossref today. "Shows" is what the paper supports; "Doesn't show" is where the app must not overreach.

| # | Design element | Claim the app makes | Paper | DOI | Shows | Doesn't show |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The ladder: learn a real skill over weeks | Sustained practice of a new skill changes adult brain structure | Draganski et al. 2004, *Nature* | 10.1038/427311a | 3 months of juggling → grey-matter increase in motion-processing areas; receded when practice stopped | Any effect on general ability; anything from short bouts |
| 2 | Same | Practice changes white matter too | Scholz et al. 2009, *Nat Neurosci* | 10.1038/nn.2412 | 6 weeks juggling → white-matter change (DTI) | Benefits beyond the skill |
| 3 | Same, over years | Effortful real-world learning drives structural change | Woollett & Maguire 2011, *Curr Biol* | 10.1016/j.cub.2011.11.018 | London taxi trainees who qualified showed hippocampal change; those who failed didn't | Transfer: trainees did worse on some other spatial tasks |
| 4 | "The edge": the app keeps you where you can't quite do it | Plasticity is triggered by a sustained gap between demand and current ability | Lövdén et al. 2010, *Psych Bull* | 10.1037/a0020080 | The supply–demand mismatch framework; mismatch must be prolonged and not too large | A specific % or minutes; it's a theoretical framework |
| 5 | Difficulty held near ~85% correct | Learning is fastest at roughly 85% accuracy | Wilson et al. 2019, *Nat Commun* | 10.1038/s41467-019-12552-4 | Derived for simple learning algorithms, matched in some human/animal data | That 85% is the rule for complex human skills |
| 6 | Hard-but-doable beats easy | Difficulties that slow performance now improve retention later | Bjork 1994, in *Metacognition* (MIT Press) | 10.7551/mitpress/4561.003.0011 | The "desirable difficulties" principle (spacing, testing, interleaving) | Brain-level change |
| 7 | Full attention required; no autopilot | Cortical change needs attention to the task, not just exposure | Recanzone, Schreiner & Merzenich 1993, *J Neurosci* | 10.1523/jneurosci.13-01-00087.1993 | Owl monkeys: auditory map expanded only when the sound was attended for a task | Human data; it's a primate study |
| 8 | One rung a day, not binge | Spacing practice across days beats massing it | Cepeda et al. 2006, *Psych Bull* | 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 | Meta-analysis of 254 studies: distributed practice improves retention | Skill learning specifically; it's verbal recall |
| 9 | Recall rungs (Field feed) | Retrieving beats re-reading | Roediger & Karpicke 2006, *Psych Sci* | 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x | Testing yourself improved week-later retention over restudy | Anything about the brain |
| 10 | Paths fade overnight; sleep counts | Sleep consolidates the day's learning | Walker & Stickgold 2004, *Neuron* | 10.1016/j.neuron.2004.08.031 | Review: sleep after learning stabilises and enhances memory and skill | That a bedtime replay in an app adds anything |
| 11 | Movement lights the brain too | Aerobic exercise grows the hippocampus in adults | Erickson et al. 2011, *PNAS* | 10.1073/pnas.1015950108 | 1 year of walking → ~2% hippocampal volume increase, older adults | Younger adults; short-term effects |
| 12 | Learning a real, complex skill (not drills) | Sustained engagement in demanding new activities improves memory | Park et al. 2013, *Psych Sci* | 10.1177/0956797613499592 | Older adults learning quilting/photography 15 h/week for 3 months improved episodic memory; passive activities didn't | Younger adults; smaller doses |
| 13 | Honesty line: games mostly improve the game | Brain-training evidence is weak for far transfer | Simons et al. 2016, *PSPI* | 10.1177/1529100616661983 | Review: strong for trained tasks, little for everyday cognition | — |
| 14 | Same | Six weeks of online brain training didn't transfer | Owen et al. 2010, *Nature* | 10.1038/nature09042 | 11,430 people: trained tasks improved; untrained tests didn't | — |
| 15 | Same | Meta-analytic consensus | Sala & Gobet 2019, *TICS* | 10.1016/j.tics.2018.10.004 | Cognitive training does not enhance general cognition | — |

## What the evidence supports, in one line each
- Learning a hard, real skill over weeks changes the adult brain and the skill. (1, 2, 3, 12)
- Change needs a prolonged gap between what you can do and what's asked. (4)
- Keep it hard but doable; attend fully; spread it across days; retrieve rather than reread; sleep on it. (5–10)
- Movement is a plasticity input in its own right. (11)
- Drill-style games mainly improve the games. (13–15)

## Gaps the app must say out loud
- Most structural findings are months of practice at hours per week. A five-minute rung is a habit, not that dose.
- The strongest human studies are in older adults or specific skills. Working professionals aged 30 are under-studied.
- No study tests "a new skill every ten days". Novelty cycling is a design inference, not a finding.
- The brain visual depicts practice. Nothing in the app measures brain change.
