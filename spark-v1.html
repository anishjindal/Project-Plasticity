    Why this works →
    Stimulate vs. Challenge

    Wiring pushes what you can already do. Spark gives your brain something new.

    • Hearing and moving are wired together.Making music links the brain’s hearing and movement systems. Learning a new rhythm exercises that link in a way your brain hasn’t practised.Zatorre, Chen & Penhune, 2007 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience
    • New skills change the brain.Adults who learned to juggle showed grey-matter growth within three months. Older adults who spent three months learning demanding new skills, like digital photography or quilting, improved their memory more than people who kept to familiar activities.Draganski et al., 2004 · Nature · Park et al., 2014 · Psychological Science
    • Timing is a skill, not a gift.Musicians tap with much less timing error than non-musicians. Precision comes from practice, which is why you can watch your taps tighten.Repp, 2005 · Psychonomic Bulletin & Review
    • The guide fades on purpose.Following a click is easy. Holding the pattern without it makes your brain build the rhythm itself, and that’s the part that has to change.
    Five days to hear with your hands

    Non-musicians learned to play a short piano piece by ear over five days. Afterwards, just listening to that piece lit up movement areas of their brains. A piece they hadn’t learned did much less.

    Lahav, Saltzman & Schlaug, 2007 · Journal of Neuroscience
    What it can’t do, honestly
    • You’ll get better at what you practise.Whether it spreads into everyday life is still being studied. This is a training activity, not a medical test.Simons et al., 2016 · Psychological Science in the Public Interest
