    The research behind today’s session

    Why each part is there.

    • You recalled, you didn’t reread.Every round makes you pull the pattern back out. Practising recall builds stronger memories than seeing the same thing again.Roediger & Karpicke, 2006 · Psychological Science
    • Words made the tiles easier.Once you spotted the word, five tiles became one chunk. That’s how chess masters remember boards and how SF the runner held 79 digits.Chase & Simon, 1973 · Cognitive Psychology · Ericsson, Chase & Faloon, 1980 · Science
    • It got harder when you got good.Two in a row and the pattern grew or sped up. One miss and it eased off. That kept you at your edge, getting most right but not all, which is where learning tends to be fastest.Wilson et al., 2019 · Nature Communications
    • Training like this changes brain activity.Weeks of working-memory practice increased activity in the frontal and parietal areas that hold information in mind.Olesen, Westerberg & Klingberg, 2004 · Nature Neuroscience
    • Tomorrow, not tonight again.People who practised a finger-tapping sequence and then slept were about 20% faster the next day, with no extra practice. Spreading practice over days also beats cramming.Walker et al., 2002 · Neuron · Cepeda et al., 2006 · Psychological Bulletin
    People whose brains changed with practice
    An athlete

    SF, the runner

    Went from 7 digits to 79 by turning numbers into race times. Same brain, new strategy, hundreds of hours.

    Ericsson, Chase & Faloon, 1980 · Science
    Working professionals

    London taxi drivers

    Years of learning the city’s streets went with a larger back part of the hippocampus, a region involved in navigation. The longer they’d driven, the larger it was.

    Maguire et al., 2000 · PNAS
    Musicians

    Concert pianists

    The more they had practised as children, the more developed the white matter (the brain’s myelinated wiring) in tracts linked to playing.

    Bengtsson et al., 2005 · Nature Neuroscience
    Adults learning something new

    Three months of juggling

    Adults who learned to juggle showed grey-matter growth in areas that track motion. After they stopped, it partly went back.

    Draganski et al., 2004 · Nature
    The rule behind it all

    “Cells that fire together wire together.”

    Carla Shatz, neuroscientist, summing up Donald Hebb’s 1949 idea · Shatz, 1992 · Scientific American
    What it can’t do, honestly
    • You’ll get better at what you practise.Memory training reliably improves the trained task and ones close to it. Clear evidence that it spreads to everyday life is still missing.Melby-Lervåg & Hulme, 2013 · Developmental Psychology · Simons et al., 2016 · Psychological Science in the Public Interest
    • That’s why we build from your world.The closer practice is to what you actually do, the more of it carries over. This is a training exercise, not a medical test.
